Fashion Jewelry Sourcing Agent in China: Buying Coordinated Accessory Collections

Plan wholesale fashion jewelry sourcing from China: materials, finishes, samples, colour variants, retail cards and supplier order coordination.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Published October 19, 2026

A fashion jewelry collection can include necklaces, bracelets, earrings and rings that share a colour or design theme. For retailers and importers, the purchasing challenge is keeping materials, finishes, sizes and packaging consistent across every SKU. Similar catalogue photographs do not establish that two products have the same construction.

How can a fashion jewelry sourcing agent help?

A sourcing agent can coordinate supplier research, comparable quotations, samples, branding, order follow-up and shipment preparation. ROYAL UNION supports China-side B2B procurement within an agreed service scope. This guide focuses on fashion jewelry and decorative accessories; precious-metal and gemstone products need additional specifications and specialist verification.

Specify the base material and finish separately

Describe the base material, surface treatment, colour and dimensions for each model. Words such as “gold colour” describe appearance and should not be confused with precious-metal content. Ask suppliers to explain exactly what they are quoting and record any proposed alternatives separately.

For necklaces and bracelets, define length, extension and clasp construction. For earrings, record the post, closure and pair configuration. For rings, specify the sizing system and whether the design is adjustable. Include decorative components, attachment details and weight where relevant to your buying decision.

Build the collection around a clear variant plan

Give each design and finish its own SKU. Record quantities by variant and ask whether minimum orders apply to the model, colour, finish or retail card. A quoted minimum for a mixed order may still require a minimum for every design.

Decide whether you are buying individual pieces, matched pairs or coordinated sets. Specify the exact contents of every sellable pack. A bracelet and necklace sold together may need different packaging from the same pieces sold individually, even when the products themselves are unchanged.

Review samples together, not only one by one

Place the proposed collection side by side under consistent lighting. Review colour consistency, surface appearance, dimensions, closures, attachment points and visible edges against the written brief. Keep an approved reference and record unresolved differences before production.

Claims such as “nickel-free,” “hypoallergenic,” “waterproof” or a specific precious-metal content need suitable supporting evidence. Requirements depend on the actual product and destination. Ask a qualified specialist to confirm the relevant assessment and documentation instead of relying on a catalogue claim or a report for another model.

Agree on packaging that protects the finish

Define whether items are individually bagged, attached to cards or packed in boxes. Consider how chains are separated and how surfaces are protected during handling. Approve card dimensions, hanging holes, barcode placement and product descriptions before printing.

For sets, create a packing reference showing the correct components and their positions. For earrings, verify the agreed pair configuration and closure count. Mixed cartons should identify each SKU clearly so receiving staff can reconcile the order without opening every retail pack.

Make branding and design approval traceable

Provide artwork you own or are authorised to use, and record its version. Do not assume a supplier's catalogue image proves permission to reproduce a brand, character or distinctive design. If ownership is uncertain, resolve the issue before committing to tooling, packaging or production.

Ask the supplier to disclose changes to components, finish processes or manufacturing arrangements that affect the approved specification. For a reorder, confirm the reference version and review any changes before accepting the previous sample as the production standard.

Buyer questions

Can several accessory styles be combined in one order?

Supplier minimums and component availability must be confirmed for each style. A coordinated range can involve several suppliers, with consolidation agreed separately.

Does gold-coloured jewelry mean real gold?

No. Describe and verify the base material and surface treatment separately. Product descriptions should match the supported material specification.
