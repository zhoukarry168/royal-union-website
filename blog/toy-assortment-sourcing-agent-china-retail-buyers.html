Toy Sourcing Agent in China: Planning Multi-SKU Toy Orders for Retail Buyers

Plan multi-SKU toy sourcing from China: age positioning, model records, sample approval, testing documents, packaging and mixed-order shipment checks.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Published October 19, 2026

Buying a toy assortment introduces a coordination problem: every model may have different components, packaging, age positioning and documentation. A supplier's catalogue is a starting point, but it is not a complete purchasing record. Retailers and importers need a model-by-model brief before approving an order.

What does a toy sourcing agent do for a mixed order?

A toy sourcing agent can coordinate supplier research, comparable quotations, sample handling, order follow-up, agreed checks and consolidation. ROYAL UNION supports these China-side activities within an agreed scope. Product compliance decisions require the applicable destination requirements and evidence for the exact product; using an agent does not replace those decisions.

Give every toy a traceable product record

Assign one buyer SKU to each model and variant. Record the supplier's model number, photographs, materials, dimensions, contents and proposed age positioning. Identify features such as batteries, magnets, detachable parts or liquids for further review instead of hiding them inside a general “assorted toys” description.

Age positioning should reflect the product and applicable assessment, not a label chosen to simplify sourcing. Before ordering, ask a qualified testing provider or relevant specialist to confirm the requirements for your product and intended market.

Separate a range decision from a supplier decision

First define the assortment: product types, quantity by model, pack format and intended customer. Then compare which suppliers can meet that brief. A factory offering more catalogue models is not automatically the right source for every toy in the order.

Request minimum quantities by model and variant. For a mixed carton, specify whether it contains a fixed assortment or random colours and designs. Random assortments can create problems when your online listing promises a specific version. Put the agreed assortment in the purchase specification.

Connect samples and documentation to the same version

Record the sample's model, materials, components, artwork and packaging version. When a supplier provides a report, check the manufacturer, model identifiers, scope and tested version, with specialist help where needed. A generic statement that a factory has certificates is not enough to establish that the selected product meets your requirements.

If materials, components or construction change after sample approval, review whether further assessment is needed. Keep the decision and supporting documents with the relevant SKU. Avoid carrying approval automatically from one model or production version to another.

Approve packaging before the final production stage

Confirm the product description, age information, instructions, required warnings, responsible-party details and traceability information applicable to the destination. The buyer and relevant specialists should verify the required content before print approval. Do not copy text from an unrelated toy or assume that translating a label is sufficient.

  • Individual pack: correct model, contents, artwork and agreed information.
  • Assortment carton: exact quantities of each model or variant.
  • Master carton: SKU identification, counts and handling information.
  • Document register: the product versions covered and any unresolved requirements.

Agree on shipment release criteria

Define which checks and documents must be complete before shipment approval. Inspection may cover identity, quantity, visible workmanship, function within agreed methods and packaging accuracy. It does not substitute for required testing or regulatory assessment. Record unresolved findings and who is authorised to approve corrective action.

For several suppliers, use a receiving schedule and carton reconciliation sheet. If one product is delayed or has unresolved findings, decide whether to wait, remove it or ship approved items separately. Confirm the impact on costs and timing before changing the shipment plan.

Buyer questions

Is a supplier's report enough to approve every toy?

No. Its scope must be checked against the exact product, version and relevant destination requirements. Ask a qualified specialist to resolve any uncertainty before ordering.

Can toys from different suppliers share a shipment?

Where the products and logistics arrangements permit, consolidation can be planned. Each SKU still needs its own purchasing record, required documentation and agreed release status.
