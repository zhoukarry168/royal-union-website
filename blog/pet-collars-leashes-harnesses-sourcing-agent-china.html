Pet Supplies Sourcing Agent in China: Buying Collars, Leashes and Harnesses as a Range

A B2B procurement guide for pet collars, leashes and harnesses: size specifications, hardware, sample checks, SKU quantities and retail packaging.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Published October 19, 2026

A matching collar, leash and harness collection creates several purchasing decisions at once: sizing, materials, hardware, colours and pack formats. Product photographs rarely show enough detail to compare suppliers accurately. A written range brief helps pet retailers and distributors request quotations that describe the same product.

How can a pet supplies sourcing agent help?

A pet supplies sourcing agent can coordinate supplier research, quotation comparison, samples, order follow-up and shipment preparation. For collars, leashes and harnesses, the useful starting point is a specification for each size and construction. ROYAL UNION supports B2B sourcing from China within an agreed service scope; product suitability and performance claims need evidence for the actual item.

Specify measurements instead of relying on S, M and L

Size labels are not a shared measurement system. State the adjustable range, strap width, leash length and relevant harness dimensions. Include a drawing showing how measurements are taken. Two suppliers may use “medium” for different dimensions or constructions, making a price comparison misleading.

Identify the intended animal and use. A walking accessory, restraint product and decorative accessory should not be treated as interchangeable. Tell suppliers what you expect the product to do, and identify any performance claims that need independent assessment.

Break the product into components

Record the webbing or fabric specification, stitching, buckle, adjustment slider, ring and clip. Ask suppliers to list proposed changes rather than substituting components silently. Hardware that looks similar in a photograph may differ in dimensions, finish, operation and construction.

  • Collars: adjustment range, buckle operation, ring attachment and edge finish.
  • Leashes: length, handle construction, clip operation and attachment details.
  • Harnesses: adjustment points, strap layout, fasteners and measurement method.
  • Matching sets: colour reference, component consistency and packing arrangement.

Plan a sample review that goes beyond appearance

Check measurements, stitching, component fit, adjustment and visible finish against the brief. Record the approved sample version with photographs and written exceptions. Functional review should follow agreed criteria; visual inspection alone does not establish strength or suitability for a particular animal.

If a product is marketed with a load rating or other performance claim, agree on the evidence required before printing it on packaging. Confirm that any test documentation relates to the proposed model, materials and relevant requirements. A report for another product should not be presented as proof for your order.

Manage the size-and-colour purchasing matrix

Each added colour and size can create another order line. Ask for minimum quantities per model, size, colour and branded pack. Separate intended order quantities from supplier minimums, then decide which variants belong in the first range. Do not assume every colour must have the same quantity unless that is your buying decision.

For sets, specify whether the collar and leash are packed together or sold individually. Confirm barcode allocation and the list of contents so that warehouse counts and retail descriptions agree.

Keep packaging and replenishment records consistent

Approve the measurement chart, product description, use instructions and care information before printing. Required warnings and labelling depend on the product and destination, so provide the applicable requirements for review. Record carton quantities and SKU labels to prevent sizes or colours being mixed at receiving.

For repeat orders, retain the approved specification and sample reference. Ask the supplier to disclose material or hardware changes before production. A previous successful order does not automatically verify a changed version.

Buyer questions

Can different pet accessory suppliers be consolidated?

Yes, where agreed receiving, labelling, readiness and shipping arrangements make this practical. Confirm the service scope and costs before committing.

Can a catalogue photo prove product quality?

No. Use a documented specification, reviewed samples and agreed checks. Claims about strength or performance require appropriate supporting evidence.
