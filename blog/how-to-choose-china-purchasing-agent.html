How to Choose a China Purchasing Agent: 12 Questions Before You Pay a Deposit

A purchasing agent can make China sourcing easier to control—or create another layer of uncertainty. Use evidence, not promises, to decide who should manage your suppliers, money, quality and shipment.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Backed by MU Group · Published August 20, 2026

The short answer

To choose a reliable China purchasing agent, verify five things before paying a deposit: who the contracting company is, how it earns money, how it selects and controls suppliers, what evidence it creates for each order, and how problems are handled. Ask for a sample quotation, specification sheet, quality checklist, receiving record and responsibility matrix. Then test the process on a controlled project before scaling.

A polished website, a low service percentage or a long supplier list does not prove execution ability. The strongest evidence is a clear connection between the buyer brief, approved sample, production order, inspection criteria, warehouse receipt and final shipment.

What does a China purchasing agent actually do?

A purchasing agent represents the buyer on agreed tasks in China. Depending on the written scope, that may include product research, supplier communication, quotation comparison, samples, order follow-up, quality-check coordination, warehouse receiving, multi-supplier consolidation, export preparation and logistics handoff.

The terms purchasing agent, sourcing agent, buying agent and procurement company are often used interchangeably. The title matters less than the operating scope. One provider may only introduce a supplier; another may coordinate the complete order. Buyers should compare deliverables, exclusions and evidence—not labels.

12 questions to ask before appointing a purchasing agent

1. What exact legal company will sign the contract?

Request the company's Chinese legal name, Unified Social Credit Code, registered address and the name of the entity receiving payment. Compare those details across the business licence, contract and bank account. China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System provides public registration and credit information, but a registry result is only the beginning of due diligence—not proof of service quality.

If a salesperson presents one company name while the contract or payment instruction shows another, ask for the relationship in writing before proceeding.

2. How is the agent paid?

Ask whether compensation is a percentage, fixed project fee, service package, margin within the goods, supplier-side payment or a combination. A low headline rate may exclude samples, inspection, storage, domestic freight, consolidation, export work or issue handling. Compare the total scope and total expected cost.

Also ask whether the agent receives commissions or rebates from suppliers. The important point is not a single business model; it is whether incentives and conflicts are transparent enough for the buyer to judge supplier recommendations.

3. How do you distinguish a manufacturer, trading company and marketplace seller?

Each supplier type can be suitable. A factory may offer production control, a specialist trading company may consolidate a category, and a marketplace seller may be useful for samples or low-volume discovery. The agent should identify the supplier's actual role rather than automatically presenting every seller as a factory.

Ask what current evidence supports the conclusion: registered business scope, address, product focus, production information, quotation behavior, samples, audit findings or other project-specific records.

4. Will quotations be compared on the same specification?

Three prices are not comparable when they describe different materials, dimensions, components, packaging or trade terms. A professional comparison should keep the buyer's critical requirements visible and mark every assumption.

At minimum, request product reference, material, size, weight where relevant, quantity, MOQ, unit-price basis, packaging, sample terms, production timing, domestic delivery, service items and quotation validity.

5. Who converts my request into a written product specification?

Photos and links help discovery, but production needs structured instructions. Ask who records the approved material, dimensions, finish, components, function, logo, artwork, packaging, barcode, shipping marks and destination-market information.

The specification should have a version or approval date. Otherwise, the buyer, agent, supplier and inspector may each be working from a different reference.

6. How is the sample approved and linked to mass production?

A sample is useful only when the approved details can be repeated. Ask how the agent records the sample version, supplier, date, deviations and buyer approval. Confirm whether a retained sample, photos, measurements or signed specification will be available for later comparison.

A purchasing agent should never describe a sample as a guarantee. The sample is a control reference; production still requires follow-up and agreed checks.

7. What quality checks are included—and what is not included?

“We check quality” is too vague. Confirm the inspection stage, sampling basis, test items, acceptance criteria, reporting method and decision authority. Some orders need only warehouse receiving and visual checks; others may require pre-production review, during-production follow-up, pre-shipment inspection or independent laboratory testing.

The required level depends on product risk, quantity, destination market and buyer tolerance. A responsible agent should state limitations rather than promise zero defects.

8. How are payments released and supplier changes controlled?

Ask who receives buyer funds, who pays suppliers, what documents support each payment and which milestones trigger release. Confirm how changes in price, material, quantity, packaging or delivery date require buyer approval.

Never rely only on a messaging-app instruction for a changed bank account. Use a previously agreed verification channel and written authorization process.

9. What happens when goods arrive at the warehouse?

For multi-supplier orders, receiving is a critical control point. Ask whether the warehouse records supplier, purchase order, carton count, visible condition, arrival date and discrepancies. Confirm storage terms, handling fees, identification labels and the process for shortages or damaged cartons.

Consolidation should combine controlled orders—not hide unresolved differences inside one shipment.

10. Who handles export documents, freight and destination compliance?

Clarify which party prepares the commercial invoice, packing list, shipping data and any agreed supporting documents. Confirm whether the agent is arranging transport directly or coordinating with the buyer's freight forwarder.

A purchasing agent is not automatically a licensed customs broker, testing laboratory or legal adviser. For U.S. imports, CBP explains that the importer of record retains responsibility for using reasonable care and meeting applicable requirements. Buyers in every destination should identify the responsible importer and obtain product-specific advice where needed.

11. What is the process when an order is late, short or non-conforming?

Ask for the escalation path before a problem occurs. Who records the issue? Who compares it with the approved specification? Who negotiates rework, replacement, credit or another remedy? Who decides whether shipment can proceed?

No agent can promise that every supplier problem will disappear. A reliable partner should provide timely evidence, preserve the buyer's decision rights and pursue the agreed resolution.

12. Which records will I receive?

At the end of a controlled order, the buyer should not be left with only chat history. Depending on scope, the project file may include:

  • Buyer brief and approved product specification.
  • Supplier shortlist and like-for-like quotation comparison.
  • Sample record and approval.
  • Order, packaging and artwork versions.
  • Production follow-up and issue log.
  • Inspection criteria and findings.
  • Warehouse receiving or carton reconciliation.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and shipment handoff information.

A practical five-stage test before you scale

  1. Send one complete brief. Include product, quantity, destination, packaging, timing and known requirements.
  2. Review the questions you receive. Good questions reveal whether the agent understands the application and commercial risk.
  3. Inspect one comparison. Check whether quotations use the same specification and expose assumptions.
  4. Approve a sample or controlled first order. Test communication, records, receiving and issue handling.
  5. Scale only after the process works. Add suppliers, categories or volume without removing the controls that proved useful.

The U.S. Commercial Service advises companies to conduct their own background research even when reviewing service-provider directories. That principle is useful for every buyer: treat recommendations as a starting point and verify the partner against your own project.

Frequently asked questions

China purchasing agent FAQ

How do I choose a reliable purchasing agent in China?

Verify the contracting company, compensation model, supplier process, specifications, samples, quality criteria, warehouse controls, export responsibilities and issue process. Review real project documents and test the workflow before scaling.

What is the difference between a sourcing agent and a purchasing agent?

The terms overlap. Sourcing emphasizes finding and comparing products or suppliers; purchasing often includes quotation, order, payment and receiving coordination. Compare the written scope rather than the title.

How can I verify a Chinese purchasing company?

Ask for the exact Chinese legal name and Unified Social Credit Code, then compare the business licence, contract and receiving account with public registry information. Continue with commercial, operational and reference checks relevant to your project.

Should a purchasing agent disclose supplier commissions?

The buyer should understand the agent's compensation and any supplier-side payment or related-party arrangement that could influence selection. Put the agreed model in writing.

Can an agent guarantee compliance?

No responsible agent should guarantee automatic compliance across products and markets. An agent can coordinate supplier information, documents and testing while each responsible party retains its own duties.

What belongs in the quotation?

Include the specification, quantity, MOQ, price basis, packaging, sample terms, timing, domestic and service costs, quality scope, warehouse and export assumptions, payment milestones and validity period.

Should I start with a trial order?

When commercially practical, a sample or controlled first order can test the complete process before a larger commitment.

What information should I send first?

Send product photos, links, drawings or samples; materials and dimensions; quantity; target price basis; packaging; destination market; timing; and known documentation requirements.
