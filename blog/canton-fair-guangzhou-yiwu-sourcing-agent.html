Canton Fair Guangzhou and Yiwu Sourcing Agent: Turn Supplier Discovery into an Order Plan

Use the fair to meet suppliers and see products. Use a structured Yiwu follow-up process to compare the same requirement, approve samples, manage the China-side work and prepare a consolidated shipment.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Backed by MU Group · Published September 28, 2026

The short answer

The Canton Fair in Guangzhou is useful for meeting manufacturers, reviewing samples and identifying possible products. A Yiwu sourcing agent can then support the agreed China-side follow-up: organize supplier records, request comparable quotes and samples, coordinate quality checks, receive goods, consolidate multiple suppliers and prepare the export handover.

ROYAL UNION is an independent sourcing and supply-chain partner, not an official Canton Fair agent. Official registration, invitation and buyer-badge services belong to the Fair's own channels.

140th Canton Fair: dates and product fit

The official Canton Fair schedule lists the 140th session in Guangzhou across three phases: October 15–19, 2026 (Advanced Manufacturing), October 23–27 (Quality Home Life), and October 31–November 4 (Better Life). The Fair's official site also provides overseas-buyer pre-registration, invitation and badge application services.

For many ROYAL UNION buyer projects, Phase 1 is relevant to hardware and tools; Phase 2 to homewares, gifts, building and furniture; and Phase 3 to toys, personal-care tools and office stationery. Check the official event directory for the current exhibition section before finalizing travel and meeting plans.

Before Guangzhou: decide what success looks like

A visit is more productive when the buyer does not arrive with only a broad product category. Prepare one comparison brief for each priority product or range:

  • product photo, drawing, sample or model reference;
  • application, target market and sales channel;
  • required material, dimensions, finish, function and set contents;
  • quantity by SKU, desired price basis and packaging;
  • brand, artwork, label, document or testing requirements already known;
  • sample deadline and target shipment window.

A practical rule: if a supplier cannot answer these fields, treat the meeting as discovery—not a ready-to-place order.

At the Fair: collect evidence, not just business cards

For each supplier, record the company name, booth location, contact person, product model, photos, material and dimensions, MOQ, price basis, sample terms, packaging, stated production timing and any available product documentation. Ask the supplier to send the quotation and specification in writing after the meeting.

Do not assume that a display item, a verbal price or a catalogue photo describes the exact version you need. The supplier may show different materials, configurations, packing levels or MOQ tiers. Note every difference so it can be confirmed during follow-up.

After Guangzhou: connect fair leads with Yiwu sourcing

Many international buyers need a route beyond the exhibition hall. The next step is to put fair contacts, Yiwu market findings and other suitable factories into one comparison process. ROYAL UNION can support the China-side stages that are agreed for the project:

  1. Clean the lead list. Group contacts by product family and compare them against the original brief.
  2. Request like-for-like quotations. Ask each shortlisted supplier to confirm the exact product, material, price basis, MOQ, packaging, sample and timing.
  3. Coordinate sample decisions. Use an approved sample and written changes as the production reference.
  4. Control the agreed order plan. Keep supplier, product, quality criteria, packaging, dates and open decisions visible.
  5. Receive and consolidate goods. When included in scope, goods from suitable suppliers can be received in China, reconciled and prepared for combined shipment.
  6. Prepare export handover. Confirm commercial documents, carton data and logistics handoff with the buyer's freight forwarder or agreed logistics route.

Why combine Guangzhou discovery with Yiwu follow-up?

Guangzhou gives buyers concentrated access to exhibitors across many industries. Yiwu is useful when a project also needs market-based product research, multi-category range building, supplier comparison, samples or ongoing coordination. The value is not a promised “secret” supplier network; it is one working record that keeps the buyer's requirement, supplier answers, sample approval and final order aligned.

This is especially practical for retailers, brands, e-commerce sellers, importers and distributors who meet several potential suppliers at the Fair but need one China-side team to continue the discussion after travel ends.

Questions to ask before appointing a sourcing partner

  • Which work is included: research, supplier comparison, samples, order follow-up, quality coordination, warehouse receiving, consolidation or export preparation?
  • How are supplier quotes and project fees separated?
  • Who approves the sample, product specification, packaging and shipment?
  • What evidence will be shared before an order moves to the next stage?
  • Which destination-market requirements remain the buyer's responsibility?

Buyer questions

Can a Yiwu sourcing agent help after I visit the Canton Fair?

Yes, subject to the agreed project scope. A sourcing partner can organize fair leads, request quotations and samples, coordinate quality checks, receive goods and prepare agreed consolidation or export handover.

Is ROYAL UNION an official Canton Fair agent?

No. ROYAL UNION is an independent sourcing and supply-chain partner. Use official Canton Fair channels for registration, invitation and buyer-badge services.

What should I collect from suppliers at the Canton Fair?

Record supplier identity, booth location, product model, material, size, MOQ, price basis, samples, timing, packaging and stated document information; then confirm important terms in writing.

Can Guangzhou and Yiwu sourcing be combined?

Yes. A buyer can combine exhibition discovery with Yiwu market or supplier follow-up when every supplier is compared against the same written product brief.
