Purchasing agent · Cost and scope

China Purchasing Agent Fees: Commission, Service Scope and Total Sourcing Cost

The cheapest fee is not always the lowest-cost sourcing route. Compare what the agent controls, what the quotation excludes and which costs can still change before shipment.

ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Backed by MU Group · Published August 24, 2026

The short answer

There is no universal China purchasing agent fee. Agents may charge a percentage of purchased goods, a fixed project fee, a service package, an hourly or day rate, a margin within quoted goods, or a hybrid model. The meaningful comparison is not the headline rate. It is the total sourcing cost for the same scope.

Before paying a deposit, ask for a written quotation that separates product cost, agent compensation, samples, China domestic transport, quality work, packaging, warehousing, consolidation, export preparation and international logistics assumptions. ROYAL UNION provides project-specific quotations because product risk, supplier count and operating work differ from one buying programme to another.

What determines a China sourcing agent's cost?

A single-product repeat order and a new multi-category retail launch do not create the same workload. The first may involve one approved specification and one supplier. The second may require product research, dozens of comparable quotations, samples, packaging decisions, multiple suppliers, receiving records and consolidated shipping.

Five variables usually shape the service scope:

  • Product complexity: standard items, custom products, packaging and private-label work require different coordination.
  • Supplier count: more suppliers create more quotations, payments, production milestones, receipts and consolidation records.
  • Quality risk: inspection stages, measurement criteria, testing and issue follow-up depend on the product and destination.
  • Order pattern: one shipment, repeated orders and an ongoing product programme need different account management.
  • Delivery responsibility: warehouse receiving, export documents, consolidation and logistics handoff may be included, optional or handled by another party.

No model proves that an agent is reliable. A transparent percentage can be clearer than an undefined “free” service, while a fixed fee can become expensive if important work sits outside the scope. Compare like for like.

Calculate total sourcing cost—not only commission

Use this buyer-side framework before comparing quotations:

Total sourcing cost

Goods + purchasing-agent fee + samples and development + China domestic delivery + inspection or testing + packaging and labels + receiving and storage + consolidation and export preparation + international logistics assumptions

This is a comparison framework, not an accounting rule. Some items may be combined, optional or paid directly to another provider. The quotation should show what is included and what can still change.

Seven fee questions to ask before paying

1. What exactly is included?

List the deliverables: supplier research, comparable quotations, samples, specification records, order follow-up, inspections, warehouse receiving, consolidation, documentation and logistics coordination. Avoid relying on the phrase “one-stop service” without a written definition.

2. What is charged separately?

Ask about sample purchases, sample freight, market visits, factory audits, laboratory testing, photography, artwork, packaging development, domestic freight, storage days, repacking and inspection travel. Separate charges are not automatically unreasonable; undisclosed charges are the problem.

3. Does the agent receive supplier-side compensation?

Understand whether supplier rebates, commissions or related-party arrangements may influence product recommendations. The commercial relationship should be clear enough for the buyer to judge potential conflicts.

4. Which value is used to calculate a percentage?

If the fee is percentage-based, confirm whether it applies to goods only or also packaging, domestic transport, moulds, samples or other costs. Confirm the currency and exchange-rate basis.

5. Is there a minimum fee?

Low-value orders can require substantial coordination. Ask whether a minimum service amount, supplier minimum or project threshold applies before spending time on product selection.

6. When is the fee earned and paid?

Connect payment milestones to defined work: research, quotation approval, purchase order, sample approval, production follow-up, warehouse receipt or shipment. Clarify refund and cancellation conditions before work begins.

7. How are scope changes approved?

New products, extra suppliers, specification revisions and repeated sampling can increase work. Use a written change process so both sides know when an additional charge requires buyer approval.

What a useful purchasing quotation should show

A quotation is more valuable when another team member can understand it without reading weeks of messages. At minimum, request:

  • product reference, photo or drawing;
  • material, size, finish, components and key specification;
  • quantity, MOQ and unit-price basis;
  • sample, tooling, packaging and branding terms;
  • supplier or sourcing-route identification appropriate to the project;
  • production and quotation-validity assumptions;
  • agent scope, fee basis and exclusions;
  • quality-check and testing assumptions;
  • warehouse, consolidation and export-preparation items;
  • payment milestones and change-control process.

Three warning signs in a low-fee offer

  • The products are not equivalent. A lower quotation may use different material, weight, components or packaging.
  • The service boundary is invisible. “Free inspection” or “free warehouse” is meaningless without inspection criteria, quantity limits, storage period and exclusions.
  • The incentive is unclear. If the buyer is not paying an obvious fee, ask how the provider earns money and whether that affects supplier selection.

A higher fee is not proof of quality either. The deciding evidence is whether the agent creates a controlled link from buyer brief to quotation, approved sample, purchase order, quality criteria, warehouse receipt and shipment.

A better way to request a quote

Do not send only “What is your commission?” Send a short purchasing brief:

Product/category: photos, links, drawings or current sample
Specification: material, dimensions, colour, function and packaging
Quantity: units, cartons, mixed-container plan or repeat forecast
Destination: country and sales channel
Commercial target: current buying price or target basis
Timing: sample and required shipment dates
Required work: sourcing, OEM, quality checks, consolidation and delivery coordination
Evidence: known testing, labelling or document requirements

Frequently asked questions

How much does a purchasing agent in China charge?

There is no universal fee. The quotation depends on the service scope, order value, supplier count, product complexity, sample work, quality checks, warehousing, consolidation and export coordination. Compare the written scope and total expected cost rather than one headline percentage.

Is a percentage fee better than a fixed purchasing fee?

Neither model is automatically better. A percentage may scale with order value, while a fixed or project fee may be easier to budget. Buyers should compare incentives, included work, exclusions, minimum charges and how changes are priced.

What should a China purchasing agent fee include?

The written scope should state whether it includes product research, supplier communication, quotation comparison, samples, order follow-up, quality checks, warehouse receiving, consolidation, export preparation and logistics coordination.

Are purchasing-agent fees included in the product price?

Sometimes they are shown separately and sometimes compensation is included within another quoted amount. Buyers should ask for a clear cost structure and disclose any assumptions needed to compare offers on the same basis.

What information is needed for an accurate sourcing quotation?

Send product photos, links or drawings, specifications, expected quantity, destination country, target price basis, packaging and branding requirements, required timing and any known testing or document needs.
