Yiwu and Guangzhou Sourcing Agent: Who Manages Your Order?

Choose Yiwu and Guangzhou sourcing support with clear responsibilities for supplier comparison, samples, order follow-up, quality checks and consolidation.

By ROYAL UNION Sourcing Team · Published October 22, 2026

You have spoken to suppliers in Guangzhou, collected product ideas in Yiwu and saved more quotations than you can easily compare. Now comes the important question: who will turn those conversations into a clear, manageable purchase order?

A sourcing agent should help you make decisions and keep responsibilities visible. The value is not simply knowing a market or introducing a supplier. It is helping your team define what is being bought, compare offers on the same basis and follow the agreed steps after the initial meeting.

Choose the sourcing route around the product, not the city label

For buyers visiting Guangzhou, the starting point may be a Canton Fair meeting, an existing supplier or a product showroom. For buyers visiting Yiwu, it may be a market category, a mixed retail assortment or a supplier shortlist that needs further investigation. Neither route is automatically right for every order.

Tell your agent what you are trying to achieve: a new furniture collection, a household-goods range, a repeat order or a set of products for online sale. Include the destination and selling channel. Then decide which meetings, market visits or remote research tasks are necessary. A useful route removes unnecessary stops instead of filling every day with appointments.

If your trip includes the autumn fair, our 140th Canton Fair sourcing plan covers the phase dates and preparation steps. For a market-focused visit, start with our Yiwu market guide.

Agree on responsibilities before asking for quotations

“One-stop service” can mean different things to different companies. Ask what is included in the proposed scope and which tasks remain your responsibility. This is particularly important when suppliers, receiving locations and shipment arrangements are spread across more than one city.

  • Supplier research: who identifies candidates and checks that the business and product match the brief?
  • Quotation comparison: who records specification, quantity, packaging and delivery basis?
  • Samples: who orders, labels and tracks sample versions, and who gives final approval?
  • Production follow-up: who requests progress updates and escalates changes?
  • Quality checks: what will be checked, when, against which criteria and with what reporting?
  • Receiving and shipping: who reconciles cartons, prepares information and coordinates the logistics handoff?

Ask for service charges, exclusions and third-party costs in writing. Do not assume that storage, testing, transport or a factory visit is free because it appears in a general service description. Clear responsibilities make it easier to compare agents and avoid disagreements later.

Use one specification across Guangzhou and Yiwu suppliers

A quotation is only comparable when the product configuration is comparable. Two chairs may look similar while using different frames, upholstery or packaging. Two household products may differ in weight, material or components even when their photographs look almost identical.

Create a brief with product references, dimensions, materials, colour, order quantity, packaging and destination. Mark unresolved details as questions. Ask each supplier to quote against that same version and identify any proposed alternative clearly.

Keep minimum quantities separate by model, colour and packaging design. Record sample costs and quoted production timing separately from your desired delivery date. A target schedule is a request, not a confirmed supplier commitment.

Make sample approval a recorded decision

Before approving production, decide what the sample must demonstrate and how it will be identified. Keep photographs, measurements, packaging references and written comments together. If you approve a colour but not the construction, make that distinction explicit.

For products with destination-specific requirements, define the documents and specialist checks needed for the exact model. An agent can coordinate information and testing arrangements within the agreed scope; that does not mean every product is automatically compliant or that an inspection guarantees no defects.

Plan mixed orders before the last carton is ready

Combining products from several suppliers can be useful, but it needs a receiving plan. Ask for carton quantities, measurements, weights and readiness dates early. Decide how shortages, damage, packaging changes and late suppliers will be recorded and resolved.

Discuss whether products are compatible for the intended shipment and which documents are required. Do not assume that moving every order to the same warehouse will always be the lowest-cost option. The right arrangement depends on the goods, supplier locations, destination and agreed logistics responsibilities.
